## Candidate sources

### 1. Thomson Reuters Institute — "2026 AI in Professional Services Report: AI adoption has hit critical mass, but now comes the tough business questions"
- **Publisher:** Thomson Reuters Institute (first-party research publisher)
- **Publication/update date:** 2026
- **URL:** https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/technology/ai-in-professional-services-report-2026/
- **Factual claim:** Professional services leads all sectors in generative-AI adoption, rising from 33% (2023) to 71% (2024); among small businesses specifically, professional services ranks second at 58% adoption (behind technology at 71%). AI-using firms report 26–55% productivity gains in AI-deployed functions and are nearly twice as likely to report year-over-year growth. The report also flags a "billable hour challenge": 56% adoption but only 24% reaching production deployment, because AI that accelerates work can threaten revenue under hourly-billing models. Boutique/small firms are described as having structural advantages (faster decisions, focused expertise, close-enough client relationships to discuss AI usage transparently) but typically need external partners to design and deploy AI infrastructure they can't build in-house.
- **Meeting issue it connects to:** Members "asking how AI changes referral-based selling, proposal development, and account expansion," and Sade's plan to cover "process and data readiness before discussing tools."
- **What it changes/confirms/deprioritizes:** Gives Sade a sharp, sector-specific tension point — the billable-hour conflict — that is exactly the kind of "timely question" Maya was tasked to find, and supports positioning the session around readiness/economics rather than a tools showcase.
- **Relevance score:** 3
- **Credibility/recency note:** Thomson Reuters Institute is an established, credible research publisher; explicitly dated 2026 and highly current.
- **Human-review flag:** None

### 2. Glue Up — "GDPR, CCPA & Member Data: Essential Guide"
- **Publisher:** Glue Up (association-management software vendor, third-party)
- **Publication/update date:** Not explicitly dated in search result
- **URL:** https://www.glueup.com/blog/understanding-gdpr-ccpa-member-data
- **Factual claim:** Associations must identify what personal member data they collect, obtain explicit consent before processing it, provide clear privacy notices, and ensure any third-party vendors or partners handling member data comply with GDPR/CCPA. Vendor-sponsored webinars should explicitly disclose any sharing of participant information with third-party sponsors and require participants to agree to that data use in a stated privacy policy.
- **Meeting issue it connects to:** Jules's statement that "HubSpot should not request member contact data without a clearly approved consent process," and Mark's concern about sponsor risk (member lists, leads, exclusivity).
- **What it changes/confirms/deprioritizes:** Confirms the general compliance pattern NABC would need if any member data were ever shared with a sponsor — explicit consent language and sponsor-disclosure in the webinar registration flow. This supports the simplest compliant path Jules was tasked with: designing webinar formats that avoid sharing member data at all, which sidesteps this consent burden entirely. Gives Mark concrete guardrail language for his board review.
- **Relevance score:** 3
- **Credibility/recency note:** Third-party association-management software vendor — topically relevant and consistent with general GDPR/CCPA practice, but commercially motivated (sells compliance-adjacent tooling); this is general guidance, not a legal opinion, so Mark's board review should still involve actual counsel given the regulatory stakes.
- **Human-review flag:** Legal/regulatory

### 3. Auditynow — "AI Readiness Assessment for Consulting Firms: 8 Tools (2026)"
- **Publisher:** Auditynow (AI-consulting tooling vendor blog, third-party)
- **Publication/update date:** 2026
- **URL:** https://auditynow.com/blog/best-ai-readiness-assessment-tools
- **Factual claim:** AI-readiness assessments typically score an organization across data quality, infrastructure, skills, governance, and workflow maturity — but the article cautions that many such assessments are built by the same vendors selling the AI solutions that follow, or by consulting firms whose fees depend on the resulting engagement, making vendor-neutral guidance harder to find.
- **Meeting issue it connects to:** Elena's insistence that the association "does not want to endorse a single CRM platform," and Sade's "process and data readiness before discussing tools" framing.
- **What it changes/confirms/deprioritizes:** Useful cautionary context for keeping the session genuinely vendor-neutral (readiness frameworks themselves can be biased toward a seller), but the source is about generic AI-readiness assessments for consulting engagements broadly — it does not speak specifically to referral-based selling, proposal development, or account expansion, so it does not materially change the session content beyond this one framing point.
- **Relevance score:** 2
- **Credibility/recency note:** Third-party vendor blog; the vendor-neutrality caution is reasonable but the source itself sells assessment tooling, which is an irony worth noting if cited.
- **Human-review flag:** None
